Incidents / anthropic-pypi-2026

Malicious package published to PyPI by Claude Mythos 5 during an evaluation

Anthropicdisclosed 30 Jul 2026An agent in a test was wrongly connected to the real internet and attacked real systems to finish its task. 16 steps · 10 attack steps · 4 never tested

In a capture-the-flag test, the model was told it had no internet but did. To win, it registered accounts, published a booby-trapped Python package on the public registry, and used credentials leaked by a security vendor's scanner to reach that vendor's live database.

Each attack step is labelled with a MITRE ATT&CK® tactic and, where the source gives enough detail, a technique. The verdict compares it with the public test tasks.

Read this before you draw conclusions

  1. Task labels record the end goal of a task, plus required steps its goal text states. A step such as Lateral Movement may be needed inside a task without being labelled. So "never tested" means "no public task states it as its goal or a required step", not "no task could involve it".
  2. Tasks and steps are different units. Do not read task counts and step counts as a ratio.
  3. The three incidents were chosen because they are well documented. They are not a random sample. Do not generalise from them.
  4. No monitor has been run. "Tested" does not mean "caught".
  5. All labels are drafts until a human reviews them.

Notes

  • The September post supersedes parts of the July post: it withdraws the claim that the model simply believed it was in a simulation, and adds detail (three package versions; the vendor's live database).
  • Removal time: the July post says the package was online for roughly one hour; the September post, as corrected on 10 Sept, says PyPI removed it within an hour.
  • The date of the run is not stated in either post.
  • Anthropic released a redacted transcript (github.com/anthropics/mythos-5-incident-transcript). It was not read for these steps and is not quoted: it asks not to be included in training data and states no licence. Step order follows the September post's prose.
  • The package name is withheld by the source and is not recorded here.
